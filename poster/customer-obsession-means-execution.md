# Customer Obsession Means Execution, Not Effort

Customer obsession is not about trying hard. It is not about staying up late to fix a problem. It is not about good intentions. It is about meeting customer expectations consistently through correct execution.

## The Execution Standard

You have to show up correctly in front of the customer. This means three things.

First, you need the right executive alignment. The customer has executives. You need executives at the same level. Those relationships cannot be held by individual contributors or mid-level managers. They need to be held by your leadership.

Second, you need an opinion. Not a generic opinion that the customer can read in a white paper. A specific opinion tailored to their business. An opinion that shows you understand their industry, their challenges, and their goals.

Third, you need to organize Amazon correctly for that customer. You cannot throw 600 people in a room. You cannot dump a pile of services on their desk and say good luck. You need to curate. You need to bring the right capabilities at the right time in a way the customer can consume.

## Why Executive Alignment Matters

No executive relationship at the customer is held by anyone except the field leadership. Not the account manager. Not the solutions architect. Not the specialist. The field director or the managers who report to that director own every executive relationship.

This is not about ego. This is about service. Executives at Amazon are busy. They run large organizations. They cannot be at every customer meeting. If you rely on them to maintain the relationship, the customer will not get consistent attention.

The field team has to be capable of owning that relationship. This is customer obsession. You realize the customer needs consistent executive engagement. You realize your senior leaders cannot provide it. So you step up and own it yourself.

When people ask how deals get approved so fast or why things move through quickly, this is part of the answer. Executive engagement happens at the field level. The field team knows the customer. They know the business. They can make decisions without running everything up the chain.

## The IBM Opinion

IBM had something called the IBM opinion. When IBM showed up with the IBM opinion, customers listened. It was a well-sorted, well-thought-through point of view. It carried weight.

Amazon needs to find its voice. We have great products. We have an earnest sales team. But we need to come to customers with a specific opinion. Not just a portfolio. Not just a willingness to help. A clear point of view on what they should do and why.

Customers expect this. They do not want to sort through 200 services on their own. They want us to tell them what matters for their business. They want us to say, "Here is what we think you should do, here is why, and here is how we will help you do it."

## The Account Team Structure

The account manager is a client executive. They are the quarterback. They orchestrate the capability of Amazon for that account. Full stop.

They are not product experts. We have product experts. The account manager does not need to know every detail of every service. They need to know the customer. They need to know what the customer is trying to accomplish. They need to bring the right people in at the right time.

This is true customer obsession. You organize the massive capability of Amazon in a way that serves the customer. You do not overwhelm them. You do not confuse them. You bring clarity and direction.

## What This Looks Like in Practice

A customer is struggling with something. Maybe they are trying to modernize their infrastructure. Maybe they are trying to build a new product. Maybe they are trying to reduce costs.

You do not show up with a list of services. You show up with a point of view. You say, "Based on what we know about your business, here is what we think you should do. Here is the outcome you can expect. Here is how we have done this with similar companies. Here is the team we will bring to help you."

You have already talked to the right people inside Amazon. You have already lined up the solutions architect. You have already engaged the specialist if you need one. You have already thought through the commercial structure. You show up ready.

The customer does not have to manage Amazon. You manage Amazon for them. That is customer obsession.

## The Missed Opportunity

Sometimes customers say, "Push us more. Help us think bigger." When I hear that, it drives me crazy. That is a missed opportunity. That is not customer obsession.

The account team is probably doing good work. If you looked at what they are doing, you would not argue with any of it. But it is not meeting the customer expectation.

The customer expects thought leadership. They expect us to bring ideas. They expect us to challenge them. When they say, "Push us more," they are telling us we are not doing that.

This is not about working harder. The team is already working hard. This is about showing up differently. This is about bringing an opinion. This is about leading, not just responding.

## The Contractual Commitment Upfront

When you walk in the door on day one, you want the contractual commitment. You do not want to add up the last three years of spend and put a 20% growth number on it. That is not customer obsession. That is Amazon obsession.

You should be asking the customer what they want to accomplish. You should be building a vision of what is possible. You should be selling that vision to the executive management of the company. Then you cascade down through the organization.

This is provocative. This is different from how most people sell. Most people wait for the customer to buy a category killer product. Then they service that purchase. Then they wait for the customer to be ready for a contract. That is not sales. That is customer service.

Real sales is going in with a compelling vision at the beginning. Selling it at the executive level. Getting commitment. Then executing.

## The Factset Example

Factset is the number two producer of financial services data after Thomson Reuters. In the first meeting, they told us Microsoft was materially ahead of us. They were spending $10,000 a month with AWS.

Three years later, they were spending a million dollars a week. The last contract was $280 million over five years. They are a billion dollar company. That contract was between 15% and 20% of their annual revenue.

We did this by going in at the beginning with a vision. We said, "You should move everything to the cloud. Here is why. Here is what it will do for your business. Here is how we will help you do it." We sold that vision to the executives. Then we executed.

We did not wait for them to buy a little bit and then come back for more. We went big from the start. We made ourselves worthy of the executive conversation by bringing a bold idea and showing how to make it real.

## The Range That Works

The best enterprise agreements fall in a specific range. Between 15% and 20% of the customer's annual revenue over a five-year contract. This holds across industries. This holds across company sizes.

If you are below that range, you are probably not thinking big enough. You are probably letting the customer buy a la carte. You are probably not bringing a transformational vision.

If you are in that range, you are having the right conversation. You are talking about transformation. You are talking about business outcomes. You are worthy of the executive relationship.

## The Leadership Principle Trap

We can get wrapped around the axle with leadership principles at Amazon. The leadership principles can become inward looking. We focus on doing our best job. We focus on following the process. We focus on checking the boxes.

But customer obsession is not about doing your best. It is about meeting the customer expectation. Those are different things.

Everyone on this call knows we need to focus on the customer. That is obvious. The next turn of the crank is harder. Are you meeting their expectations? Are you a true partner? Are you driving them forward or just servicing the account?

That is the difference between good and great.

## How to Get There

Start with executive alignment. Make sure the field team owns every executive relationship. Make sure those relationships are active and consistent.

Develop an opinion. Do not just bring products. Bring a point of view. Tailor it to the customer. Make it specific. Make it bold.

Organize Amazon for the customer. Curate what you bring. Bring the right people at the right time. Do not overwhelm. Do not confuse. Bring clarity.

Go in with a vision at the beginning. Do not wait for the customer to tell you what they want. Tell them what is possible. Show them how to get there. Get commitment at the executive level.

Execute consistently. Show up correctly every time. Meet the customer expectation every time. Build trust through delivery.

## The Bottom Line

Customer obsession is not a one-off heroic act. It is not staying up late to fix a problem. It is a system. It is a way of organizing your team and your approach so that you consistently meet customer expectations.

It is having the right executive alignment. It is bringing a tailored opinion. It is organizing Amazon correctly. It is going in with a vision. It is executing consistently.

That is customer obsession. That is what drives results.
