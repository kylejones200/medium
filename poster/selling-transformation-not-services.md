# Selling Transformation, Not Services

Most sales teams wait for customers to buy products. They service those purchases. They wait for the customer to be ready for a contract. This is not sales. This is customer service. Real sales means going in with a vision and selling transformation from day one.

## The Contractual Commitment Upfront

Walk in the door on day one and ask for the contractual commitment. Do not add up the last three years of spend and put a 20% growth number on it. That is Amazon obsessed, not customer obsessed.

Go in with a provocative idea. Sell it to the executive management of the company. Then cascade down through the organization.

This is controversial. People will tell you it is not the Amazon way. They will tell you customers need to start small and grow. They will tell you it is too aggressive.

They are wrong. The results speak for themselves.

## The Factset Story

Factset is the number two producer of financial services data after Thomson Reuters. In the first meeting, they told us Microsoft was materially ahead of us. They were spending $10,000 a month with AWS.

We did not accept that. We went in with a vision. We said, "You should move everything to the cloud. Empty out every data center. Here is why. Here is what it will do for your business. Here is how we will help you do it."

We sold that vision to the executives. We got commitment. Then we executed.

Three years later, Factset was spending a million dollars a week with AWS. The last contract was $280 million over five years. They are a billion dollar company. That contract was 28% of their annual revenue.

We took them from $10,000 a month to a million dollars a week because we went in with a transformational vision from the start.

## The 15% to 20% Rule

The best enterprise agreements fall between 15% and 20% of the customer's annual revenue over a five-year contract. This holds across industries. This holds across company sizes.

We ran the data. We looked at the top 20 deals. They all fell in this range. A billion dollar company should sign a contract between $150 million and $200 million over five years. A $10 billion company should sign a contract between $1.5 billion and $2 billion over five years.

If you are below this range, you are not thinking big enough. You are letting the customer buy a la carte. You are not bringing a transformational vision.

If you are in this range, you are having the right conversation. You are talking about transformation. You are talking about business outcomes. You are worthy of the executive relationship.

## Why This Works

Going in with a big vision does two things.

First, it gets you out of the bizarre negotiating for a rug. It gets you out of line-item pricing discussions. It takes you up a level where you are talking about business transformation.

Second, it makes you worthy of the executive conversation. If you are asking for $100 million instead of $20 million, you need to explain why. You need to show the vision. You need to show how it transforms the business.

This forces you to do the work. You have to understand the customer's business. You have to understand their challenges. You have to build a compelling case. You have to show how AWS enables outcomes they cannot achieve today.

## The Provocative Idea

The provocative idea is not about products. It is about outcomes. What can the customer accomplish if they fully commit to cloud?

Maybe they can launch products faster. Maybe they can reduce time to market from 18 months to 6 months. Maybe they can handle 10x the transaction volume. Maybe they can enter new markets. Maybe they can reduce infrastructure costs by 40%.

The idea has to be big enough to matter. It has to be specific enough to be credible. It has to be tailored to their business.

You do not show up with a generic cloud pitch. You show up with a specific vision for their company based on what you know about their industry, their challenges, and their goals.

## Selling at the Executive Level

This only works if you sell at the executive level. You cannot sell a $100 million transformation to a director of infrastructure. You have to sell it to the CFO, the COO, or the CEO.

This means you need executive alignment from day one. You need to get access. You need to build the relationship. You need to earn the right to have that conversation.

Once you have access, you need to bring a point of view. You need to say, "Here is what we think you should do. Here is why. Here is what it will enable. Here is how we will help you get there."

You are not asking them what they want to buy. You are telling them what is possible and why it matters.

## Cascading Down

After you get executive commitment, you cascade down through the organization. You work with the infrastructure teams. You work with the application teams. You work with the security teams. You build the detailed plan.

But the commitment comes first. The executive says yes to the vision. Then the organization figures out how to execute.

This is the opposite of how most people sell. Most people start at the bottom and try to build up. They get a small win. They try to expand. They hope it eventually reaches the executive level.

That approach is slow. It is incremental. It does not create transformation.

## The Objection: This is Not Amazonian

When you propose this approach, people will say it is not Amazonian. They will say Amazon believes in starting small and growing. They will say customers need to experiment before they commit.

This is a misunderstanding. Amazon believes in customer obsession. Customer obsession means giving the customer the best outcome. For enterprise customers, the best outcome is transformation, not incremental adoption.

Starting small makes sense for startups. It makes sense for companies that are new to cloud. It does not make sense for a Fortune 500 company with a billion dollars in revenue and a clear need to modernize.

Those customers do not need to experiment. They need a partner who can help them transform. If you show up with a small pilot, you are not that partner.

## The Risk

The risk is that you ask for too much and the customer says no. This is a real risk. But it is a smaller risk than you think.

If you have done the work, if you have built the relationship, if you have tailored the vision to their business, they will not say no. They will negotiate. They will ask questions. They will push back on details. But they will not walk away.

And if they do say no, you learn something valuable. You learn they are not ready for transformation. You learn they are not the right customer for this approach. You can adjust.

But most of the time, they say yes. Because most executives want transformation. They want a partner who can help them get there. They want someone who brings a bold vision and shows how to make it real.

## The Execution Standard

Once you get commitment, you have to execute. You have to deliver on the vision. You cannot sell transformation and then deliver incremental projects.

This means you need the right team. You need solutions architects who can design the target state. You need specialists who can solve specific problems. You need project managers who can keep things on track. You need account managers who can orchestrate all of this.

You also need executive engagement from AWS. You need your leadership to stay involved. You need them to show up for quarterly business reviews. You need them to escalate issues when needed.

The customer committed to transformation because you sold a vision. You have to deliver on that vision.

## The Advertising and Marketing Example

In the advertising and marketing segment, we did not have an AWS-specific solution. But we did have Amazon Ads. The problem was that AWS and Amazon Ads did not coordinate.

We started from a blank sheet of paper on January 1st. We forged a go-to-market with Amazon Ads in a very short time. We brought in $125 million to $130 million of new business at IPG. We brought in $100 million of Adobe business separately.

This was an aha moment. We should be doing a lot more of this. We should be bringing the full power of Amazon to these accounts, not just AWS.

We also realized that software and internet companies want to be sold to as product companies, not as portfolio companies. They want vertical integration. They want solutions tailored to their products.

We reorganized our approach. We stopped throwing Legos across the desk. We started coming in with specific solutions for specific products. This was new for these teams. They had never done it before. But it worked.

## The Permission Question

People often ask, "Who gave you permission to do this?" The answer is no one. We did not wait for permission. We saw what the customers needed. We organized Amazon to deliver it. We just did it.

This is ownership. You do not wait for someone to tell you the right approach. You figure it out. You test it. You adjust. You scale it.

If you wait for permission, you will wait forever. If you act and deliver results, no one will ask why you did not wait for permission.

## How to Start

Pick a customer who is ready for transformation. Not a customer who wants to experiment. A customer who has a clear business need and the budget to address it.

Build the vision. Understand their business. Understand their challenges. Understand what they could accomplish if they fully committed to cloud. Make it specific. Make it compelling.

Get executive access. Do not try to sell this to a director. Sell it to the CFO, the COO, or the CEO. Build the relationship. Earn the right to have the conversation.

Sell the vision. Go in with confidence. Say, "Here is what we think you should do. Here is why. Here is what it will enable. Here is how we will help you get there."

Get commitment. Ask for the contractual commitment upfront. Do not wait. Do not start small and hope to grow. Go big from the start.

Execute. Deliver on the vision. Bring the right team. Stay engaged. Solve problems. Build trust through delivery.

## The Bottom Line

Selling transformation is different from selling services. It requires a bigger vision. It requires executive engagement. It requires confidence. It requires execution.

But it delivers bigger results. It creates real partnerships. It drives customer outcomes. It grows revenue faster.

Stop waiting for customers to buy products. Stop servicing small purchases and hoping they grow. Go in with a vision. Sell transformation. Get commitment upfront. Then execute.

That is how you win big deals. That is how you create real value for customers. That is how you grow.
